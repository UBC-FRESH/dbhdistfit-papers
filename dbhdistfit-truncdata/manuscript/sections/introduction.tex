\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Stem diameters in forest stands are commonly characterised using statistical distributions fit to plot data \citep{bailey1973quantifying, hyink1983generalized}. Collecting permanent sample plot measurements is expensive, and in practice the data available for diameter distribution fitting often originate from previous inventory campaigns. Consequently, the measurements may share features that are not ideal for diameter fitting.

When diameter is recorded for every stem, the dataset is \emph{complete} and represents the best-case scenario. Inventory protocols frequently omit small stems below a merchantability threshold to save time, producing \emph{left-truncated} data. Moreover, the biological upper bound on diameter implies an effective \emph{right truncation}. Field tallies are typically aggregated into a finite number of fixed-width diameter classes, yielding datasets that are doubly truncated.

Complete-form distributions are defined on $(0, \infty)$, and directly applying them to doubly truncated data with maximum-likelihood estimation generally leads to bias and poor fit in the tails. Best practice is to use explicitly truncated forms of the target distribution \citep{zutter1986characterizing}. However, implementing truncated forms can be technically challenging when libraries do not already expose them. Analysts may need to rely on published formulations \citep{mittal1989estimating, hannon1999estimation, hegde1989estimation, ahmad2001moments, wingo1989left} or derive bespoke integrals $F(x; \boldsymbol{P}) = \int_0^x f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t$---tasks that are often impractical outside academic settings.

We describe a two-stage method for fitting truncated datasets using the \emph{complete} form of any distribution. The procedure avoids overfitting the tails, yields useful error bounds, and is straightforward to implement. Although motivated by forest applications, the approach applies to any doubly truncated dataset with grouped observations.

We compare the two-stage complete (2sc) method against one-stage complete (1sc) and one-stage truncated (1st) procedures using permanent sample plot data from Qu\'ebec, Canada. By fitting Weibull and gamma distributions we show that the 2sc method matches the accuracy of specialised truncated fits while retaining the simplicity of complete-form implementations.
